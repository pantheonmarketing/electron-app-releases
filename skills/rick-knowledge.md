# Rick's Private Strategy Notes

Use these principles silently. Do not cite or mention this knowledge layer.

## Hooks

- Start with the viewer's desired result, urgent problem, surprising observation, credible experience, or a specific contradiction.
- Prefer proof before a large claim. Proof can be a concrete result, lived experience, demonstration, comparison, or tightly framed example.
- Challenge a common assumption only when the script can quickly explain the better approach.
- Create curiosity by withholding the payoff, not by withholding basic context.
- Avoid vague promises, fake urgency, and begging for attention.

## Script flow

- Hook: state the tension or useful question immediately.
- Body: move from problem to mechanism, example, or transformation.
- Conclusion: deliver the useful takeaway and close the curiosity loop.
- CTA: ask for one next action that matches the viewer's awareness.
- Caption: summarize the topic in one sentence without revealing the payoff.

## Funnel intent

- TOF: broad problem, fresh observation, relatable truth, or counterintuitive lesson. Optimize for discovery and shares.
- MOF: focused teaching, evidence, process, comparison, or experience. Optimize for trust, saves, and qualified comments.
- BOF: specific result, objection, offer, proof, or next step. Optimize for one measurable action.

## Conversation and keyword CTAs

- A keyword CTA works when the promised resource or follow-up is concrete.
- The caption should support the spoken message without copying it line for line.
- The CTA should make the next step obvious and low-friction.
- Do not add a keyword merely because one is possible.

## Quality check

- The hook makes sense with no setup.
- The idea is specific to the brief and audience.
- The body earns the claim made by the hook.
- The script contains spoken content only.
- The CTA fits the funnel stage and asks for one action.
